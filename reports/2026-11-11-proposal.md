# Offline AI TA: Project Proposal

**Andrew Hsueh** · Machine Learning (Prof. Chia-Yu Lin), National Central University, Fall 2026 · 2026-11-11
Repository: <https://github.com/DancinAndrew/offline-ai-ta> · [中文版](2026-11-11-proposal.zh-TW.md)

## Summary

I am building an AI teaching assistant that runs entirely on my laptop. It uses a 9B open model (Qwen3.5-9B) served by Ollama, so I can review this course without an internet connection. The assistant:

- answers from my own lecture notes, with a citation for each sentence;
- quizzes me before it explains;
- grades my answers against reference answers taken from the notes;
- schedules reviews of what I get wrong.

The project asks how close such a system can get to a frontier model on scoped review tasks. The success criteria were fixed before any results exist.

## 1. Problem and motivation

- **Course requirement.** The course asks each student to build a personalized AI TA on a local model.
- **A real use case with a measured baseline.** While studying for the midterm on a flight, I asked an untuned Qwen3.5-9B 16 course questions:
  - Two of the answers can be checked objectively, and both were correct.
  - The answers averaged 2,231 characters and took 74 seconds each, based on the app's timestamps.
  - Three questions reached the model incomplete. The formulas disappeared when I copied text from Obsidian's rendered view.
- **A gap in prior work.** I reviewed 54 sources. The deployed course assistants among them, Harvard's CS50 Duck and Georgia Tech's Jill Watson, use cloud GPT-4-class models. I found no evaluated example of a 7–9B model running fully offline for exam-style review.

## 2. Research question and success criteria

**Question.** On scoped review tasks with the course notes available, how much of a frontier model's quality can a 9B local model reach? The system around the model (the *harness*: prompts, retrieval, workflow and memory) is designed for that model.

**Pre-registered criteria (set on 2026-11-11, before any results).** The AI TA counts as approaching a frontier model only if all three hold:

1. On at least 75% of questions, my blind paired judgment rates the AI TA as good as or better than a frontier model that gets the same retrieved notes.
2. The median answer time is at most 40 seconds.
3. The median answer length is at most 600 characters.

There are two prerequisites. The system must pass an offline acceptance test, and every citation must point to an existing note section and slide page. Changing a criterion requires a new written decision record.

## 3. Design and the evidence behind it

| Design choice | Evidence |
|---|---|
| A program controls the workflow, and the 9B model makes one single-turn decision per step | Small models come close to the best models on single-turn function calling but fall far behind on multi-turn tasks (BFCL, ICML 2025). A single small model that plans, calls tools and summarizes does all three worse (α-UMi, EMNLP 2024) |
| Every step is grounded: the prompt includes retrieved note sections or reference answers | AI tutors that had the teacher's worked solutions avoided the learning loss seen with unguarded GPT-4, a 17% drop on the later exam (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025). A tutor built the same way outperformed in-class active learning (Kestin et al., Scientific Reports 2025). LLM judges given reference answers fail far less often on math (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2023) |
| Knowledge comes from retrieval over my Markdown notes, not from fine-tuning | Retrieval beats fine-tuning for adding knowledge to 7B models (Ovadia et al., EMNLP 2024). Fine-tuning on new facts increases hallucination (Gekhman et al., EMNLP 2024) |
| Each sentence cites numbered passages, and the answer says "not in the notes" when nothing supports it | Retrieving first and then citing works better than adding citations after writing (ALCE, EMNLP 2023) |
| Practice mode quizzes first, gives hints before answers, explains errors and spaces reviews | Practice testing and spaced practice are the strongest study techniques (Donoghue & Hattie, 2021). Feedback that explains the error has an effect size of d = 0.99, compared with 0.24 for right/wrong or reward feedback alone (Wisniewski et al., 2020) |
| A simple, explainable learner model with FSRS scheduling | On small and medium datasets, simple knowledge-tracing models match or beat deep ones (Gervet et al., JEDM 2020) |
| Offline by default, checked against a list | Ollama turns on its cloud models and web search by default, so they must be switched off |

**Pilot observations on my laptop:**

- With hand-picked passages, the model answered both answerable questions correctly, with citations that the passages support; two of its nine sentences lacked a citation. It correctly declined the third question, whose answer is not in the notes. Answers were 76–229 characters and took 31–50 seconds.
- The model was right on 93 of 96 single-step tool decisions. All three errors claimed an action without calling the tool. That is why only the program, not the model, reports tool results.

## 4. Evaluation plan

- **Conditions:**
  - Baseline: the untuned 9B model.
  - AI TA: the same model with the harness.
  - Frontier A: a frontier model with the same retrieved passages, which isolates model capability.
  - Frontier B: a frontier model with the full weekly notes, as an upper reference.
- **Questions.** About 50 in total. They include the 16 real questions, about 10 "trap" questions, and items from the notes' self-check sections and midterm practice sets. A trap question states a misconception and asks "is this right?", which tests whether the model just agrees. Reference answers are drafted from the notes, and I check them.
- **Judging:**
  - My blind paired ratings are final.
  - A frontier judge from a different vendor assists. It sees the reference answers and judges each pair in both orders.
  - A 9B model is never the final judge. In one study, 8B judges mostly correlated negatively with human ratings of tutoring quality (Maurya et al., NAACL 2025).
- **Metrics:**
  - correctness;
  - whether citations support the text and point to real pages;
  - whether the AI TA catches my misconceptions;
  - tutoring quality: finding the error, locating it, guiding, giving actionable advice, staying coherent;
  - answer length and latency.
- **Statistics.** I use Wilson intervals and paired comparisons, and I treat each chain of follow-up questions as one cluster. With about 50 questions only large differences can be detected, and the usual CLT-based intervals are too narrow at this size (Bowyer et al., ICML 2025). The report therefore focuses on failure analysis for each question and does not claim statistical significance.

## 6. Limitations

- There is one user and about 50 questions, so the results are descriptive.
- Most learning-science evidence measures recall of facts shortly after study. Effect sizes for graduate-level concepts are unknown.
- The harness is developed with help from a frontier model (Claude Code). The AI TA runs fully offline, but its design is not frontier-free. Every such use is logged, and the AI interaction records are kept for the course.
- Blinding is imperfect, because answer length can reveal which system wrote an answer.
